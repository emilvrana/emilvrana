AI-NOW! 2026: A Show for the General Public and One Real Talk

November 6, 2026

On Wednesday Eva and I went to AI-NOW! 2026 in Kino Lucerna. I expected a professional conference and paid for one. What I got was a well-produced show for people meeting AI for the first time: big statements about where everything is going, clear slides, a hall that had come to be reassured. It was never boring, and very little of it was interesting. In five hours I wrote down less than in one hands-on workshop at Měsíc AI last month.

The exception was Mike Bubeníček, who leads creative strategy at an agency in Sydney. He told the room he had flown some thirty hours to be there, and the talk was worth the trip. He walked through how AI is used in campaigns for global brands: how a creative idea is tied to what the campaign has to achieve, how the tools are chosen, how a brand keeps a recognisable style when much of the material is generated, and how he judges whether a result matches the brief. Real work rather than what the tools can do in principle, delivered by someone who has plainly done it many times. I do not work in marketing and still took more from his thirty minutes than from the rest of the evening. The last part, judging a generated result against the brief, is the question I ask of every pipeline I build.

My take: more a well-produced show for the general public than a pro conference, and Mike’s talk is the one that really clicked for me.

The best part of the evening was not on the programme. Eva, who is running the first AI feature at her company, argued with half the speakers under her breath, which made the first block much shorter. Afterwards we walked to a small sushi place a few streets from Lucerna, agreed on the verdict and disagreed about the agent talk, which she liked more than I did. The sushi was the best value of the night. The rest of the evening Eva took into her own hands, with a programme of her own, and I am still not quite over it.
