What Actually Changes on 2 August, for People Who Do Not Read Regulations

July 29, 2026

On Sunday, 2 August, another part of the EU's AI Act starts to apply. Friends keep asking me what will change for them, and the honest answer is: less than the headlines suggest, and not quite what they expect. The law has been arriving in stages since 2025, and in May the EU agreed to move its most demanding part back by more than a year.

What you will notice is transparency. A chatbot or a voice system that talks to you has to tell you that it is a machine, unless that is obvious. Pictures, sound and video made to look real, the so-called deepfakes, have to be labelled as generated by whoever publishes them. And the companies that build generative AI have to mark what it produces in a way software can recognise. Systems that were already on the market get a few extra months for that, until December.

What will not change yet is the part about decisions that shape people's lives: AI used in hiring, credit scoring, schools or access to public services. Those rules were due to start now and will start in December 2027 instead. My friend Eva, who works on a credit-scoring product at a fintech company, described her team's reaction as relieved and annoyed at once: relieved to have more time, annoyed to have prepared for a deadline that moved.

Two earlier parts are already in force. Since February 2025 some uses are simply banned, such as scoring citizens by their social behaviour, and since August 2025 the makers of the large general-purpose models have their own obligations. The picture below puts the dates on one line.

For most people, then, the date that matters is the one after which a phone call has to admit it is a machine. The rest waits until December 2027.
