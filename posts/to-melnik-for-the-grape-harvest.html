To Mělník for the Grape Harvest, on a Route Planned by a Machine

September 22, 2026

The grape harvest festival in Mělník is the one weekend of the season when the Saturday rides from Tom's bike workshop in Karlín fill up a week in advance. Last Saturday nine of us rode about fifty kilometres north along the Vltava, through Kralupy, to the town where the Vltava meets the Elbe, with burčák, the half-fermented young wine of the season, as the official reason.

I planned the day with an AI assistant, which is how I plan most rides now. I gave it the group's pace, the forecast and one condition: we come back by train, bikes included. It did the dull part well. It moved the start an hour earlier to fit the forecast, kept us on the river path instead of the main road, and found a train back that was not the one the whole festival would take. On a festival day that last detail is worth more than the route.

It also suggested that we lock the bikes at the racks on the main square, which on the day of the festival stood behind a stage. We found a fence. Tom, who would like AI to run his whole workshop, asked over the second burčák whether it could also stop his online booking form from losing bookings. That one is not for a model, I said, I will fix it myself, quickly. Eight people heard me say «quickly».

The festival itself was what it is every year: stalls along the streets, music from several stages, wine from the Mělník slopes and from much further away, and more people than the old town looks built for. We stayed two hours, which on a bike is the right dose, and caught the quiet train home.

My take: let the machine read the forecast and the timetable, and let people decide where to stop.
