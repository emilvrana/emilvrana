A Robot Dog for a Nine-Year-Old, and It Only Speaks English

January 20, 2026

On Saturday my nephew Matěj turned nine, and I had promised him a robot. Not a toy that does three tricks and goes into a drawer, but one he can program, ideally with some AI in it, since that is what his uncle does for a living. It also had to listen when spoken to, and that narrowed the shelf quickly.

LEGO's news from CES in Las Vegas at the start of the month tempted me for one evening, until I read that the new Smart Brick reacts to play but cannot be programmed, and that it reaches shops only in March. SPIKE Prime is the classic school robot, but LEGO no longer makes it and second-hand it costs more than a nine-year-old's birthday present should. The mBot2 was the safe choice. I bought the Bittle X, a small robot dog from Petoi that walks, sits and understands more than thirty-five spoken commands, and can be programmed with blocks, Python or C++. The maker recommends it from ten. I decided that the missing year is what an uncle is for.

The commands come in English and Chinese. Matěj told it «sedni» five times, louder each time, while the dog stood there looking polite. Then he tried «sit», it sat, and from that moment the party was held in English. By the evening he was giving orders like a football coach, his little sister was convinced the dog obeyed her clapping, which nobody corrected, and my father, who calls everything I do «the computers», asked whether it could learn Czech.

It can, a little: ten of its commands can be programmed. So the present came with homework, and the homework is partly mine. We are teaching it «sedni» together, Matěj at the keyboard in Šumperk and me on a video call from Prague. For a child the real lesson is not the voice recognition. It is finding out that the clever part is a list someone wrote, and that he can add to it.

My verdict: the best present I have bought in years, and the first one that came with homework for the person who gave it.
