Machine Learning Prague 2026: Two Days Where Nobody Promised Miracles

May 7, 2026

On Tuesday and Wednesday I was at Machine Learning Prague, the large international conference on machine learning held in Prague every spring, this year at O2 Universum, a short ride from where I live now, in Libeň. It is not an event for the general public and does not pretend to be one. The hall is full of people who train, deploy and maintain models, and the questions after the talks are about numbers rather than the future of humanity.

The talks I chose had titles that could serve as my job description: why good models fail after deployment, what to do when benchmarks are not enough, why there is no optimal configuration, how to evaluate AI agents that work on code security. The common thread, visible already in the programme, was measurement. A model that scores well in a test can still disappoint the moment real users touch it, and the speakers came from banks, telecoms and software companies, places where a disappointing model costs real money.

The part I enjoyed most was the poster hall, where Czech work stood next to the big names. One poster measured how well real-time speech recognition handles Czech; another described a foundation model built for Czech web pages. Czech is a small language for the companies that build the large models, and much of the work that makes those models usable here is done by people standing next to a poster, explaining it to whoever stops. I stopped at both and stayed longer than at most talks.

What I took home is a short list: two ways to check my clients' pipelines after release rather than only before it, and one experiment with a smaller model that I will run on our own documents before I believe anyone's chart. That is the only measure of a conference I trust: whether I do something differently on Monday.

My verdict: a professional conference in the full sense, expensive and worth it. The most common word in the hall was «measure», and nobody promised miracles.
