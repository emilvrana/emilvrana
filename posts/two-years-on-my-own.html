Two Years on My Own

March 19, 2026

Two years ago this month I left my job as a backend engineer at a Prague financial company and started working on my own, building AI systems for companies that want them to do real work rather than demos. This month I signed my longest contract so far: the rest of the year with a seven-person engineering team at a company that processes documents for a living. It seemed a good moment to look back.

The first year was small projects and a lot of explaining. Almost every enquiry began with «we want AI» and ended, when it went well, with a narrower sentence: read these invoices, sort these requests, find this clause in ten thousand contracts. In the second year the questions got better. Clients now arrive having tried a chatbot themselves, and they ask the important question earlier: what happens when it is wrong.

The figures the Czech Statistical Office published in January match what I see. Last year 18% of Czech companies with ten or more employees used some form of AI, slightly below the EU average of 20%. Yet 13% used it to write text or code, well above the EU average of 9%. Czech companies take up the writing tools quickly and slow down at the next step, where the tool has to work with their own data and someone has to answer for the result. That next step is where I earn my living.

My own work changed more than my clients' did. A coding agent now writes most of my first drafts, and my job moved from typing to reading: deciding what it should do, writing the test before the task, and throwing away about a third of what it produces. The habit that helps most is boring. One small task at a time, with the check it has to pass, and I read every line it changes. I write fewer lines than two years ago and read many more.

In short: two years in, the work is less about what AI can do and more about who checks it, and I would not go back to an employment contract.
